QA Workflows for the AI SDLC · Sudhakar Kakunuri · VibeTestQ

📖 How to use this

Timing totals: ~42–48 minutes of talk time. Trim fast-flip sections if your slot is 45 min.

Key line (yellow) = own this, memorize it, say it slowly.

Script box (blue) = verbatim lines for anchor moments.

Pause cue (purple) = deliberate silence — count it.

Warning (red) = anti-pattern or non-negotiable delivery note.

ACT 1 — THE CONFESSION

Earn trust · Admit what we got wrong
1Title Slide
⏱ 30 sec

Walk on. Don't rush. Let the title breathe 5 seconds.

OPENING "Before we start — this isn't a talk about AI. It's a talk about us. About something our industry got wrong for a long time."
Pause. Click.
2Before I begin
⏱ 90 sec — slow down

This is where you earn the room. Speak slowly. Make eye contact.

SCRIPT "I've spent 21 years in this industry. Trained 10,000+ engineers. Given 50+ keynotes. So the least I owe this room — is to tell you what we got wrong. Not AI. Us. The IT industry."
Pause 3–4 seconds. Let the posture shift in the room. Then click.
3Mistake #1 — We Chose Disconnection
⏱ 2 min

Engage before telling them the answer.

ASK THE ROOM "Quick show of hands — who uses Jira?" (most hands)
"Who uses GitHub or GitLab?" (most hands)
"Notice something? Jira ships with Bitbucket. You chose GitHub. We did this to ourselves."
💡 "We didn't inherit fragmentation. We chose it."

Don't moralize. State it as fact. Move on.

4Mistake #2 — We Never Cared About Traceability
⏱ 2 min
ASK "Honestly — when code changed last sprint, did anyone know exactly which tests to re-run?"

Wait. Let the silence confirm.

💡 "AI didn't break our SDLC. It exposed a debt we refused to pay."
Pause here. First thesis statement. Let it land.

ACT 2 — THE REVERSAL

AI is paying back the debt — but not for us
5Act 2 Divider
⏱ 15 sec

"Here's the twist. Everything we failed to build for 20 years — AI is now building. But not for us. For itself."

6The IDE is Becoming the OS of Your Project
⏱ 90 sec
ASK "Think about Cursor, Copilot, Antigravity. What do they do that VS Code from 2020 didn't?"

They're not text editors anymore. They remember intent, read the whole project, talk to Jira.

💡 "The IDE is becoming the OS of your project."
7From Code Repository → Project Repository
⏱ 90 sec

This is the conceptual shift that everything downstream depends on. Make it simple.

SAY "We used to put only code in the repo. Everything else lived elsewhere — Jira, Confluence, TestRail, Slack. AI couldn't see it. Now the repo holds the entire project. Every artifact. Version-controlled. AI-readable."

Don't belabor. Move to the next slide — where you challenge your own claim.

8Wait — Am I Saying Replace Jira?
⏱ 90 sec — POWER MOMENT

Intellectually honest moment. Audiences remember this.

SCRIPT "Wait — am I saying replace Jira? No. Your team uses Jira and TestRail today. Both things cannot be true. So which is it?"

Pause. Then answer:

ANSWER "The Project Repository doesn't replace Jira. It adds a layer on top. Jira owns the human process. The repo owns the machine context. Nothing thrown away."

This slide builds trust. People will quote this later.

9The Artifact: graph.json
⏱ 2 min
SCRIPT "Let me make this concrete. This is the file. graph.json. You commit it to your repo. AI updates it on every commit. Every query to AI reads it. Requirements, code, tests — all linked."

Point at the left panel: "This is how it's created."
Point at the right panel: "This is what a single entry looks like."

💡 "The traceability debt — paid by a file in your repo."
Don't explain every JSON field. Let them scan it.
10Documentation: Finally Getting Solved
⏱ 60 sec
ASK "How many times has your team asked developers for proper documentation? And how many times did you get it?"

Small laugh expected. Then the insight:

💡 "Documentation wasn't solved for us. It's being solved because AI needs it more than we do."

Quick slide. Move on.

ACT 3 — TYPES OF AI SDLC

Which type is your team actually running?
11Act 3 Divider
⏱ 15 sec

"Let's talk about the 5 types of AI SDLC. Each one is not just a stage — it's a different driver controlling your SDLC."

12The Types of AI SDLC — With Meaning
⏱ 90 sec — DIAGNOSTIC MOMENT

Most important slide of Act 3. Don't read the tiles aloud.

SCRIPT "Five types. Five different drivers. Look at each one — and privately note which one is actually running your SDLC right now."
Pause 5–7 seconds. Let them scan.
THEN "Now note which one you tell people you're in. Those are usually not the same."

Don't wait for confirmation. Move on — the discomfort is the teaching.

13Each Type is a Shift in the Center of Control
⏱ 45 sec
SAY "Each type is really a shift in who controls the SDLC. Humans, then tools, then specs, then workflows, then AI, then governance. Every step, the center of control moves."

Quotable line. Reframes from "maturity" to "who decides."

14Type 1 — AI-Assisted
⏱ 60 sec
ASK "Raise your hand if your team 'uses Copilot' — but your sprint ceremonies haven't changed at all."

Most hands go up.

💡 "That's Type 1. Individual speed up 30%. System speed up zero."
15Type 2 — Spec-Driven (think BDD, reborn)
⏱ 75 sec
SCRIPT "Those of us who lived through BDD a decade ago will recognize this. Given/When/Then. The idea was right. It just needed AI to actually work."

Engineers over 35 will nod. That's deliberate — validates the older audience while sounding contemporary.

💡 "It's BDD — reborn."
16Type 3 — Parallel Stream
⏱ 45 sec
SAY "This is where most teams want to be. Dev and QA starting at the same moment. No waiting. Built, tested, automated — same sprint."

Don't oversell. Most teams think they're here. Very few actually are.

17Type 4 — Agentic
⏱ 45 sec
SAY "This is the frontier. Agents plan, code, test, heal. Humans govern — they don't execute."
⚠ "The agent approved it" is not a legal defense. The human gate stays.

That line matters if anyone in your audience is in a regulated industry.

18Type 5 + Uncomfortable Truth
⏱ 60 sec — UNCOMFORTABLE MOMENT
SCRIPT "Type 5 is banking, healthcare, fintech. Where 'AI did it' is not a legal defense."

Then pause. Lean in slightly.

💡 "Most teams are Type 1 — dressed up as Type 3. And pretending doesn't help when production breaks."
Don't apologize. Don't soften. Let it sit.

ACT 4 — THE GENERATION GAP

The one idea you carry home
19Act 4 Divider
⏱ 10 sec

Longest pause of the talk coming up. Prepare yourself.

"Now — to the one idea I want you to take home."

20Why Every AI Vendor Chased Generation First
⏱ 60 sec
ASK "Every AI demo you've watched in the last 2 years — generation or validation? Now think — why?"

Let them think. Then:

SCRIPT "Generation sells itself. It fits in a 90-second demo. It funds the next round. It goes viral. Validation? Doesn't demo. Takes context. Measured by what didn't break. No viral moment."

Setting up the black slide.

21🎯 THE BLACK SLIDE
⏱ 45 sec — MIC-DROP MOMENT
DO NOT RUSH. Click to the slide. Then say NOTHING for 5 seconds.

Let the room read it. Let it become uncomfortable.

THEN, SLOWLY "Generation is 2026. Validation is 2023. Three years behind. That gap has a name. It also has an owner."
Pause again. Then click.
🔥 This is the 10-second silence moment of the whole talk. OWN IT.
22This Is QA's Moment
⏱ 60 sec

Now flip from tension to opportunity.

SCRIPT "Most people hear 'validation is 3 years behind' and get defensive. Wrong read. The gap is territory. Whoever closes it — owns the next decade of quality."
💡 "The generation gap is not a problem. It's a career."

This is your LinkedIn quote. Say it clearly.

ACT 6 — QA WORKFLOWS

11 workflows · Design · Execution · Intelligence
32Act 6 Divider
⏱ 15 sec

"Now — the workflows themselves. Grouped by purpose, not by where they run."

33A QA Workflow is Not a Test Case
⏱ 45 sec
SCRIPT "If I ask most teams for their workflows, they send me a test case template. That's not a workflow. A workflow is a loop — end to end, continuous, learns from production."
💡 "A test case fires once. A workflow runs forever."
3411 Workflows Grouped
⏱ 60 sec
SAY "11 workflows. Three groups. Design is before testing starts. Execution is during. Intelligence is what the system learns."
💡 "Design shapes quality. Execution delivers it. Intelligence compounds it."
⚠ Next 10 slides are workflow details. Announce: "I'll hit the 4 most important. Rest are in the deck for later."
35WF1 — Requirements → Scenarios
⏱ 60 sec — GO DEEP
ASK "How long does it take your team, from receiving a user story, to solid test scenarios the team agrees on?" (Most will say days.)
SAY "Minutes. If WF1 runs right. AI extracts testable conditions, flags ambiguity before dev starts, QA approves. Done."

This is the gateway workflow. Without it, nothing downstream works.

36WF5 — Automation Generation
⏱ 45 sec
SAY "Scenarios exist. Code exists. Nothing stops the test code from writing itself — if AI has both."
⚠ "More code ≠ more coverage. Always review."
37WF8 — Coverage Audit
⏱ 60 sec — MAKE THIS LAND
ASK "When code changed last sprint — how did you know your tests still covered what mattered? Or did you just trust the green suite?"
Pause. Let them squirm a little.
💡 "Green tests ≠ covered tests." — OWN THIS LINE.
38WF11 — Production Feedback Loop
⏱ 75 sec — IMPORTANT
ASK "When a bug escapes to production — what actually feeds back into your next sprint? A Jira ticket? Or nothing?"
SAY "WF11 closes the loop. Production bug → AI generates a test → added to next sprint → never happens again."
💡 "Production stops being the end of the pipeline. It becomes the start of the next sprint."
39WF2 — Exploratory Planning
⏱ 20 sec · FLIP

"WF2. Exploratory — but deliberate. AI proposes risk areas, QA explores, findings feed the next sprint."

40WF3 + WF4 — Test Data & Bug Triage
⏱ 25 sec · FLIP

"WF3 — test data generation. WF4 — bug triage. Two workflows that quietly eat sprint hours. Both solved at generation speed."

41WF6 — Test Maintenance
⏱ 25 sec · FLIP

"WF6. Test maintenance. The hidden sprint killer."

⚠ Without QA review — the suite stays green because it's gutted. Review gate non-negotiable.
42WF7 — Self-Healing Tests
⏱ 20 sec · FLIP

"WF7 — self-healing at runtime. Pipelines stay green despite UI drift. But every heal is logged. Silent fixes hide real regressions."

43WF9 + WF10 — The Quality Gates
⏱ 30 sec · FLIP

The session title, finally resolved
55Act 8 Divider

"Now — the session title. QA Workflows for the AI SDLC. Everything we've built so far comes together here."

56Current State — Parallel Disconnected Streams
⏱ 60 sec — THE CALLBACK

Callback to Mistake #1 from Act 1. Let the audience feel it.

57Integrated State — The Unified Loop
⏱ 75 sec — HERO SLIDE

This is the thesis landing.

SCRIPT "Here's what integrated looks like. Requirement to WF1 gate. AI code plus AI tests running parallel. WF5 and WF8 gates. Merge. Production. WF11 loops back."
💡 "One loop. Not two streams. QA workflows are gates — not a parallel universe."
Let it sit. This is where the session title resolves.
58Match Workflow to SDLC Type
⏱ 45 sec
SAY "Match the workflow to your type. Mismatch equals failure. Type 4 workflows in a Type 1 team collapse. Type 1 workflows in a Type 3 team leave value on the table."
💡 "Start with your actual type. Not the one on your slides."
59Evidence, Not Opinion
⏱ 90 sec — EXECUTIVE MOMENT

Slow down. This is the line your audience will quote in LinkedIn posts.

ASK "At sprint end, when leadership asks 'can we ship' — what does your team answer with today?"

Listen. Usually: "I think so." "Tests passed." "QA signed off."

SCRIPT "That's opinion. That's trust. That's hope. With these workflows, you answer with evidence."
💡 "Release decisions from evidence — not opinion." — OWN THIS ONE.
Pause. Let it land.

ACT 9 — WHERE IS THIS HEADING?

AI takes the middle · Humans anchor the ends
60Act 9 Divider
⏱ 15 sec

"Last section before we close. Where is this heading? What does the future actually look like?"

61AI Has Taken the Middle
⏱ 90 sec
SCRIPT "Look at any modern SDLC. Humans still own intent — product, architecture, risk. AI has taken execution — code, tests, optimization. Humans still own accountability — governance, production, compliance."
💡 (In AI's voice) "AI has taken the middle. You define the intent and own the outcome. The execution in between is mine."

Say that line slowly. It's a quote worth sitting with.

62The Middle is Expanding. The Ends Are Not.
⏱ 60 sec
SAY "Here's the nuance though. The middle is expanding. AI is starting to propose architectures, make operational calls. The boundary isn't fixed."
💡 "What stays with humans isn't tasks. It's accountability. Ownership."

Closing line of this slide: "AI owns execution. Humans stay anchored at intent and accountability."

63The AI IDE Becomes the Command Center
⏱ 75 sec
SCRIPT "The AI IDE becomes the command center. One environment holding everything — requirements, code, tests, diagrams, pipelines. Version-controlled. AI-maintained."
CLARIFY "Jira-PM might get replaced — because the AI IDE tracks progress from actual code. But CI/CD doesn't disappear. It's the engine running beneath."
💡 "AI IDE = interface. CI/CD = engine. PM tools = legacy."
