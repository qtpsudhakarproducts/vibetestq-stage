What is an LLM?

🎓 QA Analogy

An LLM is like a brilliant new QA joiner who has read every test strategy document, every bug report, every Jira ticket, every test automation article ever written — but has never actually opened your application.

What it CAN do

  • Write test cases from requirements
  • Explain what a function does
  • Suggest edge cases you missed
  • Generate Gherkin scenarios
  • Write bug report descriptions

What it CANNOT do (alone)

  • Know your specific application
  • Know your team's conventions
  • Verify its own output is correct
  • Remember your last conversation
  • Replace your QA judgement

Your job: be the senior QA who briefs, reviews, and guides this brilliant-but-new joiner.

How an LLM Actually Works

🎓 QA Analogy

Like preparing for a certification exam. Study millions of books (Training) → Sit the exam (Your Prompt) → Give an answer (Response). The model doesn't look things up — it answers from what it learned.

⚠️ Key fact: The model does not search the internet. It does not look at your app. It answers entirely from patterns learned during training.

What is Hallucination?

🎓 QA Analogy

That new joiner — rather than saying "I don't know", they confidently give you an answer that sounds completely right. Formatted beautifully. Totally wrong.

❌ Real QA Example

You ask AI: "Write test cases for our checkout page"

AI returns 10 perfectly formatted test cases including:

  • Assertions for a "Save for Later" button
  • A promo code field validation test
  • A loyalty points redemption flow

None of these features exist in your app.

✅ Why It Looks So Convincing

  • Correct Gherkin syntax
  • Realistic field names
  • Proper Given/When/Then structure
  • Matches e-commerce patterns from training data

It is confident because it has seen similar apps — not yours.

Why Human Verification Matters

You are the QA of the AI's output. This is not new — it is literally your job.

The Familiar Pattern

Developer writes code → QA reviews and tests it

AI writes test cases → QA reviews and verifies them

AI generates automation → QA reviews and runs it

What to Always Check

  • Does this field/button actually exist?
  • Does this test cover the real requirement?
  • Are negative scenarios included?
  • Are edge cases realistic for our users?

The Good News for QA

AI doesn't eliminate QA thinking. It amplifies it.

Instead of spending 2 hours writing test cases from scratch — you spend 20 minutes reviewing and improving what AI generated.

Your domain knowledge, your understanding of the app, your QA instinct — that is what AI cannot replace.

🎓 Think of it this way

A spell-checker doesn't make you a worse writer. It frees you to focus on the ideas.

AI as Collaborator, Not Answer Machine

❌ Answer Machine Mindset✅ Collaborator Mindset
"Write my test cases." (then copy-paste) "Here's the requirement. Here's my app context. Draft test cases — I'll review."
Trust the output because it looks professional Review every test case against the actual application
One prompt, one response, done Prompt → review → refine → review again (like a dev review cycle)
"The AI got it wrong, AI is useless" "The AI got it wrong — I need to give it more context"
Use AI to replace thinking Use AI to do the first draft — your thinking improves it

The shift: You are not a prompt copier. You are a quality reviewer who uses AI to work 5× faster.

LLM Training — How the Model Learned

🎓 QA Analogy

Like a QA engineer who studied thousands of test plans, bug databases, and automation codebases before their first day at work. All that reading became instinct — but it has a cutoff date. They don't know what changed in your app last week.

Collect Data
books · code · websites · Q&A · articles
→
Train the Model
predict next word, billions of times
→
Fine-tune
human feedback improves responses
→
Deployed Model
knowledge is now frozen

What this means for QA

  • AI knows general testing patterns very well
  • AI does NOT know your specific application
  • AI's knowledge has a training cutoff date

What you must provide

  • App context — what it does, who uses it
  • Your specific requirements and constraints
  • Examples of your conventions and standards

Context Window — AI's Working Memory

🎓 QA Analogy

Like opening a fresh Jira ticket with no description, no attachments, no history, no comments. The AI has no idea what your app does, what your team conventions are, or what you discussed yesterday — unless you tell it right now.

❌ What happens without context

"Write test cases for the checkout page"

AI invents an e-commerce checkout based on what it has seen before. Probably not your checkout.

✅ What happens with context

"Our checkout has 3 steps: cart review, address, payment. Payment accepts Visa/Mastercard only. No PayPal. Write test cases."

Now AI generates test cases for YOUR checkout.

Tokens — Why There Are Limits

🎓 QA Analogy

Like a test execution log with a maximum file size. Once it's full, the oldest entries get dropped. You can't fit your entire 200-page test plan into one AI prompt — it would exceed the limit and get cut off.

What is a Token?

AI doesn't read word by word. It reads in chunks called tokens.

test ing the check out page

Roughly: 1 token ≈ ¾ of a word. 1,000 tokens ≈ 750 words.

What this means practically

  • Don't paste your entire requirements doc — summarise it
  • Long conversations can lose early context
  • Be concise and specific in prompts
  • Break large tasks into smaller prompts
QA Tip

Instead of pasting 500 test cases — paste 5 representative examples and ask AI to follow the same pattern.

AI Ethics & Responsible Use

Using AI irresponsibly can create real problems — for your client, your company, and yourself.

🚫 Never share with AI

  • Real user data or PII (names, emails, IDs)
  • Client application URLs or credentials
  • Production test data with sensitive records
  • Proprietary business logic or trade secrets
🎓 QA Analogy

You wouldn't email your client's user database to a freelance contractor. Same rule applies to AI.

✅ Safe practice for QA

  • Use synthetic / anonymised test data
  • Describe app behaviour — don't paste real data
  • Check your company's AI usage policy first
  • Always verify AI output before using it
  • Disclose when a deliverable was AI-assisted
Good habit

In Level 2 you will learn to generate synthetic test data — so you never need to use real user data with AI.

Prompts & Context

How to Talk to AI and Get Useful QA Outputs

Role · Context · Instruction · Format · Iterative Prompting

What is a Prompt?

🎓 QA Analogy

A prompt is like a work ticket you raise for a contractor. A vague ticket gets vague work. A detailed ticket — with context, acceptance criteria, and examples — gets exactly what you need.

❌ Vague Prompt

"Write test cases for login"
  • No context about your app
  • AI invents a generic login
  • Output needs heavy rework

✅ Detailed Prompt

"Act as a QA engineer. Our login accepts email + password. 3 failed attempts locks the account for 15 minutes. Write 6 test cases covering positive, negative and boundary scenarios. Return as a Gherkin feature file."
  • Role, context, instruction, format — all present
  • AI generates for YOUR login

Anatomy of an Effective Prompt

1. Role
+
2. Context
+
3. Instruction
+
4. Format
=
Great Output

1. Role

Tell AI who to be before asking anything.

"Act as a senior QA engineer with expertise in e-commerce applications."

Same question with role vs without role produces noticeably different outputs — role sets the lens.

2. Context

Tell AI about your specific situation.

"Our checkout has 3 steps: cart → address → payment. We accept Visa and Mastercard only. Guest checkout is supported."

Context is the briefing you give a new QA joiner before asking them to test.

Instruction & Format — The Difference Makers

3. Instruction

Tell AI exactly what to do. Be specific.

"Write 8 test cases covering: happy path, empty fields, invalid email format, wrong password, account lockout after 3 attempts, and password reset link."

Vague: "Write some test cases"
Specific: "Write 8 test cases covering these exact scenarios"

4. Format

Tell AI how to structure the output.

"Return as a Gherkin feature file"
"Return as a markdown table with columns: ID, Title, Steps, Expected Result"
"Return as a numbered list"

Format is the most underused part of a prompt. It decides whether you can paste the output directly into Jira — or spend 20 minutes reformatting.

🎓 Real QA Format Examples

For Jira: "Return as a table with ID, Summary, Steps, Expected Result"  |  For automation: "Return as a Gherkin .feature file"  |  For review: "Return as a numbered list with rationale for each test"

Iterative Prompting

🎓 QA Analogy

Exactly like a QA review cycle. Dev submits code → QA reviews → raises comments → Dev fixes → QA re-reviews. You don't accept the first version. Neither should you accept the first AI output.

1. First Prompt
draft test cases
→
2. Review
what's missing?
→
3. Refine
"add negative scenarios"
→
4. Better output
review again

❌ One-shot mindset

Send one prompt. Get output. Copy-paste into Jira. Done.

This is how hallucinations end up in your test suite.

✅ Iterative mindset

  • Prompt → review → refine → review
  • Ask AI to critique its own output
  • Ask AI what it might have missed
  • Add more context when output is off

Prompt Examples — Manual Testing Tasks

Test Case Generation

// Role + Context + Instruction + Format Act as a senior QA engineer. Our registration form has: - Email (must be valid format, unique in system) - Password (min 8 chars, 1 uppercase, 1 number) - Date of Birth (must be 18+ years old) Write 10 test cases covering positive, negative, and boundary scenarios. Return as a Gherkin feature file with Scenario Outlines where applicable.

Bug Report Writing

// Turn rough notes into a professional bug report Act as a QA engineer writing a Jira bug report. Issue: Checkout breaks when user has items from 2 different sellers. Error appears on payment step. Doesn't happen with single-seller cart. Write a professional bug report with: Summary, Environment, Steps to Reproduce, Expected Result, Actual Result, Severity, and suggested root cause.

Prompt Examples — Reviewing & Improving Output

Ask AI to Review Its Own Work

// Second prompt — iterative refinement Review the test cases you just generated. Identify: 1. Any scenarios that are duplicates 2. Any critical negative paths you missed 3. Any boundary value cases not covered Then add the missing scenarios.

Ask AI to Explain Code (for QA reviewing automation)

// You don't need to read code like a developer Act as a QA engineer reviewing Playwright test code. Explain what this test does in plain English. Identify any assertions that might be missing. Suggest what additional checks a QA would want. [paste the code here]

Good Output vs Deceptive Output

AI output that looks complete is not the same as output that IS complete. This is the most important QA skill you will apply to AI.

❌ Looks great — has problems

AI returns 10 well-formatted Gherkin scenarios for your registration form.

  • 3 scenarios are near-duplicates
  • No test for duplicate email (your biggest bug risk)
  • Age validation test uses 17 years — wrong boundary
  • Password validation doesn't test the special char rule

Looks: 10/10  |  Coverage: 5/10

✅ How a QA reviewer catches this

  • Map each test case back to a specific requirement
  • Check: is every business rule covered?
  • Check: are boundary values exactly right?
  • Check: what are the top 3 ways this feature breaks?
  • Ask AI: "what negative scenarios did you miss?"

This is QA expertise — and AI can't replace it.

GitHub Copilot — AI Inside Your Editor

🎓 QA Analogy

Like autocomplete — but instead of completing a word, it completes an entire test function. It reads your intent from the comment or function name, and suggests the implementation.

Inline Suggestions

Skills.md — Solving the Context Window Problem

🎓 QA Analogy

Like your team's Definition of Done — but written for AI. It tells Copilot and Claude: always write tests in TypeScript, always use Page Object Model, always include assertions, our app URL is X, our test data conventions are Y. You write it once. AI reads it every time.

❌ Without Skills.md

  • Every new chat — blank context
  • Repeat your project conventions every time
  • AI uses JavaScript when you want TypeScript
  • AI generates flat tests instead of POM
  • Inconsistent output across sessions

✅ With Skills.md

  • AI reads your conventions before helping
  • Always TypeScript, always POM, always your patterns
  • Consistent output every session
  • Context is loaded automatically via MCP
  • Your QA standards baked into every AI output

Writing Your First Skills.md

1
Project context — what the application does, who the users are, what tech stack is used
2
Test conventions — naming patterns, folder structure, TypeScript or JS, framework used
3
AI rules — always use POM, never hardcode test data, always include assertions, always add comments
4
Examples — paste 1–2 sample test cases that match your desired style

# QA Skills for ShopEasy App ## Project E-commerce platform. React frontend. Node.js backend.
